# Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure (Seefeld, 2026)

*An eight-page essay by Leon Seefeld, "with thoughts from Indy Johar", dated 11 May 2026. Its subtitle is "Why Place-Based Systems Matter in an Age of Degenerative Volatility" (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 1). Read for this page: all eight pages.*

## What it argues

Seefeld describes a Europe where volatility and uncertainty are rising, trust is breaking and soils are losing fertility (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 1). He calls this an Age of Degenerative Volatility, in which the baselines for regenerative work are eroding (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 1). Resilience tends to be shaped in place, and Europe's strength reflects the condition of its places in aggregate (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 1).

The bioregion is the scale "where global forces become tangible, and where coherent response remains possible" (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 2). Bioregioning, the ongoing practice of restoring and stewarding place-based systems, therefore becomes strategic infrastructure (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 3).

The essay then turns to finance. The stability of returns depends more and more on foundational economies (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 5). Seefeld expects Security Economics to drive the debate more than market economics, with a focus on continuity over efficiency (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 6). Capital for bioregional resilience is foundational, like investment in transport or digital infrastructure (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 7). The last page describes Dark Matter Labs' role. It is building an operating system for bioregioning: cascading risk sensing, multi-capital models, many-to-many agreement spaces and Bioregional Financing Facilities (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 8).

## Key points

- **A bioregion is a life-place.** Climate, water, soils, biodiversity and settlement shape it. Its boundaries are functional and emerge from living systems and cultural practice (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 2).
- **The right scale.** Bioregions are large enough for systemic dynamics, yet small enough for visible feedback and for people to care (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 3).
- **Existing groundwork.** Community organisers and place-based institutions have spent years, sometimes decades, building trust and informal governance at this scale (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 3).
- **Optionality.** "Optionality is a perishable resource" (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 4). Without renewal, places are pushed "into corridors of necessity rather than horizons of possibility" (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 4).
- **A security issue.** The stake is the autopoietic capacity of place: a system's ability to reproduce, maintain and evolve itself (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 5). Stewardship institutions such as commons, trusts, Bioregional Observatories and Bioregional Financing Facilities can hold long-term commitments (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 5).
- **Foundational economies.** These are water, food, energy, health, housing, social care and transport. All other value creation depends on them (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 5).
- **A worked example.** Restoring an upstream watershed can stabilise flows, reduce flood risk and improve water quality. That protects yields and reduces insurance volatility downstream (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, pp. 6 to 7).
- **For asset allocators.** Diversification offers limited protection when underlying systems degrade (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 6). The question becomes "how early and how effectively they engage with it" (raw/[public] Bioregioning as Critical Infrastructure.pdf, p. 7).
