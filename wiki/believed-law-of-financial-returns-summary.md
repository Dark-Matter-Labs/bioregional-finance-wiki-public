# The Believed Law of Financial Returns (Seefeld, 2026)

*A three-page essay by Leon Seefeld, dated 30 June 2026 (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 1). Read for this page: all three pages.*

## What it argues

Seefeld questions the belief that capital allocation needs financial return as its main organising principle (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 1). The belief has become so familiar that it feels like a law of nature. Yet the conditions behind the old investment model keep eroding (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 1). Seen this way, the pursuit of return looks "more like a design choice inherited from a different era" (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 1).

The problem he names is plumbing. Many allocators already want stronger places, healthier watersheds and resilient food systems. Current financial infrastructure rarely gives them a direct route to those outcomes (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 2). So capital passes through structures built to recreate conventional returns, even where those returns depend on cross-subsidies (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 2).

## Key points

- **A fifth option.** Using the Five Options Framework, Seefeld sees a possibility beyond market-rate investment and philanthropy (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 2). Capital can pursue portfolios of returns that include resilience, ecological carrying capacity and social cohesion (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 2).
- **Return as one measure among several.** Financial return becomes one possible expression of value, no longer its universal measure (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 2).
- **The cost of the workaround.** Old structures "may constrain entirely new forms of capital allocation before they have the opportunity to mature" (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 2).
- **Willing capital exists.** Family offices, foundations, public institutions, pension funds and private investors increasingly see that flourishing places protect their balance sheets (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 3).
- **The missing piece.** What may be missing is financial architecture able to receive capital on those terms (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 3).
- **The closing question.** It asks whether "the financial system has built enough pipes for the capital that already wants to flow" (raw/The Believed Law of Financial Returns.pdf, p. 3).
