# BFF Discovery Arc 3: Risk and Value Sharing

*Discovery Arc 3 is the third provocation paper in a series of four on Bioregional Financing Facilities (BFFs). Its cover reads "v.3.1", last updated 15 September 2026. The paper names no author. Read for this page: all 31 pages. The mechanism tables on pages 8 to 10 extract as scrambled text, so this page names those mechanisms without detailing each one.*

## What it argues

The arc asks how projects in a bioregional portfolio should share risk and value "so that collective resilience, trust, and mutual advancement can emerge" (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 2). Conventional portfolios treat each project alone. In a bioregion the projects already depend on one another, so that logic works against the shared goal (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 2). "The portfolio is more than a container—it becomes a commons in itself." (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 2).

The paper says the design must work in three directions at once. It needs solidarity within the portfolio, reciprocity between capital and place, and responsibility toward the wider bioregion (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 3). Its closing line sums up the stance: "In such a portfolio, failure is not abandonment, and success is not isolation." (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 31).

## Key points

- **Correlation is a strength here.** Portfolio theory diversifies away correlated risk. Bioregional projects are already correlated by geography, ecology and policy, and the paper treats that interdependence as something to harness (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 3).
- **Evidence from mutual finance.** Research from microfinance and cooperative finance suggests peer support and shared accountability can lower default risk. Moral hazard tends to appear where governance is weak (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 5).
- **Mechanisms within the portfolio.** These include portfolio-level risk pools, mutual guarantees, mutual credit, shared infrastructure, steward-ownership conversion, orderly wind-down, and a "portfolio weaver" role (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, pages 8 to 9).
- **Mechanisms with investors.** These include drawdown-triggered capital, a portfolio-level first-loss layer and conditional return deferral. Others draw returns from whole-portfolio revenue, or let the portfolio buy back investor stakes over time (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, pages 9 to 10).
- **Mechanisms with the bioregion.** These include local supply networks, a multi-capital clearing system, an open resilience buffer for non-members during shocks, and shared early-warning networks (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, pages 9 to 10).
- **The Sleipnir case.** Stichting Sleipnir, a Dutch foundation founded in the 1980s, holds its member enterprises outside private ownership. Members pay 1% of turnover into a common liquidity fund, and loans from it are decided collectively (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, pages 11 and 13).
- **Sleipnir under stress.** During COVID-19 it moved staff, capital and loan relief between members, and no member went bankrupt. Investor returns are capped, and members cannot be sold for an exit (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 14).
- **Legal form is secondary.** An investment fund, a cooperative and a holding company each help and hinder sharing in different ways. The paper concludes that "the legal form alone does not determine whether risk and value are shared effectively" (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, page 20).
- **Start with relationships.** Practical advice opens with trust between members, then a clear purpose for pooling, then small, time-bound experiments (raw/[external]_v4_BFF Risk & Value Sharing_DiscoveryArc#3_15.09.2026.pdf, pages 23 to 24).

## Where it sits in this wiki

- [[BFF Discovery Arc 1: Allocation Decision Governance]] decides which projects enter the portfolio. Arc 3 asks how those projects then carry risk and value together. Arc 1 leaves open how to rebalance risk between investors and communities, and Arc 3 takes that up (synthesis).
- [[BFF Discovery Arc 2: Blended Finance and Catalytic Capital]] argues that first-loss capital should be the exception. Arc 3 keeps a portfolio-level first-loss layer as one option, which places it at the portfolio rather than the single project (synthesis).
- [[BFF Discovery Arc 4: Multi-Capital Returns]] supplies the wider idea of value that Arc 3's multi-capital clearing system would need to track (synthesis).
- [[The BFF Pattern Cards]] catalogue related instruments in card form, such as steward ownership and risk pools (synthesis).
- [[BioFi Deep Dive: bioregional financing facilities in Europe]] presents the four arcs as one Bioregional Weaving Labs programme. It also reports that portfolio weavers have proved a highly useful role in practice.
