# SI-SF: System Investing and System Finance (Codex 25)

*Codex 25 is a 74-page report from Dm Capital Systems, published in 2025 (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 1). Its named authors are Indy Johar, Leon Seefeld, Irina V. Wang and Raj Kalia. The report says it will be published in partnership with the TWIST team (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 4). Read for this page: the whole document in passes, from the contents and preface to the appendix (pages 1 to 74). Page numbers below are PDF pages, which match the printed page numbers.*

## What it argues

The report defines system investing as a strategy for changing whole systems, whether social, economic, environmental or a mix (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 4). System finance is the set of instruments, capital structures and governance models that make that strategy work (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 4). It argues that value structuring is an often overlooked bottleneck. It defines this as "organising and accounting for the value generated through large-scale interventions and converting this value to price" (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 15).

From there it builds a shared vocabulary. It covers what makes a system, value returns, investment strategies, capital structures, core functions and barriers (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 7-68). A fictional city, Ederfield, runs through the report as a worked case. The authors state that Ederfield is invented and draws on cities they work with (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 5). They describe their own frameworks as "at best, partially tested hypotheses that continue to evolve" (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 2).

In this view capital becomes a servant of the system and an enabler of other forms of capital (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 52). Metrics turn into tools for learning, and contracts become flexible agreements built on reciprocity (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 53-54). The report also treats democracy, culture, nature, rivers and bioregions as layers of quasi-infrastructure worth investing in (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 60-62). It states that it is not investment advice (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 4).

## Key points

- **What makes a system.** The report names four conditions: distributed agency, awareness of interdependency, shared purpose, and shared infrastructure for coordination (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 8). It also lists look-alikes that fail, such as a portfolio built to avoid correlation, or a monoculture held stable by subsidy (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 9-10).
- **Five layers of value return.** Input returns, direct value, spillover to nearby places, systemic outcomes like public health, and entangled macro-level outcomes (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 16-18). Pricing and sharing value across these layers is how the report proposes to make system programmes investable (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 18).
- **The Boring Revolution.** Three shifts: unbundled property rights, dynamic value chains, and structured economic systems where many actors pool resources and risk (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 19-23). Digital registries, smart contracts and algorithmic pricing form the backbone (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 25-26).
- **Four investment strategies.** Capability building, single-point investments with many beneficiaries, multi-point investments in one asset, and M3 portfolios (multi-point, multi-asset, multi-beneficiary) (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 30-33). The High Line in New York and Hawaiʻi Investment Ready's Food Systems Accelerator serve as real examples (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 30, 33).
- **Virtuous and stabilising portfolios.** Virtuous portfolios reinforce each other's effects. Stabilising portfolios buffer shocks in fragile systems and buy time for later change (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 34-37). The report suggests a portfolio of portfolios that shifts with the state of the system (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 37).
- **A 4x3 capital matrix.** Four stages of deployment meet three mechanisms: funds, transition financing facilities and monetary mechanisms (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 39). The stages run from capability building and deal construction to project financing and securitisation (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 39-40). Monetary mechanisms include new development finance institutions such as bioregional development banks (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 40). Funds alone are not enough, the report argues, and system finance often needs synthetic instruments (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 41).
- **Five core functions.** The first two are an outcome-stewards alliance of beneficiaries and an investors alliance. The others are a system or outcome accelerator, a system trust acting as public interest agent, and an independent governance and learning framework (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 45-50).
- **Barriers.** The report lists high interest rates, unpriced polycrisis risk, assets with no asset codes and bilateral contracts. It adds gaps in the capital stack, weak legitimacy and missing mandates for systems such as bioregions (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, pp. 64-66). System entrepreneurship also lacks dedicated risk capital and its own working methods (raw/DmCs_SI-SF_2708.pdf, p. 67).
