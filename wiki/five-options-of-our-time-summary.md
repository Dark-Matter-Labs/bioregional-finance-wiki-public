# The 5 Options of Our Time (Seefeld, 2026)

*A six-page essay by Leon Seefeld of Dark Matter Labs, dated 19 June 2026, with the heading "When the Frame Shifts" (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 1). Read for this page: all six pages.*

## What it argues

Seefeld starts from the familiar polarities of public debate. One side puts ecological recovery, social justice and collective responsibility first. The other side puts growth, prosperity, freedom and individual responsibility first (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 2). Over time these positions hardened into identities (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 2). Some people seek a middle path, such as green growth, ESG, impact investing or carbon pricing. Others propose different organising principles, such as gift economies, regenerative business and post-growth (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 3).

His central claim is that the frame itself can shift. He suggests that civilizational resilience may become the main organising principle of the coming decades. In a world of shocks and fragmentation, "continuity itself may become the overriding concern" (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 3). Older questions then give way to questions about regional food systems, energy under stress and trust in institutions (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 4). He calls this Option 5 and describes it as "a different game altogether" (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 4).

He then applies the idea to finance. Under what he calls a Security Economics frame, attention moves to foundational economies such as watersheds, energy systems, food security and social cohesion (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5). He closes by naming the hardest part as psychological: people become attached to the debates themselves (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5).

## Key points

- **The framework.** It builds on the tetralemma of Buddhist and Jain logic, which offered four positions: this, that, both and neither (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 6). Seefeld adds a fifth option, "Shift the Frame", which makes the original question and options obsolete (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 6).
- **The five options in order.** This, That, Something in Between, Something Else, and Shift the Frame (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 6).
- **Option 5 follows a change of context.** Options 1 and 2 oppose each other, option 3 seeks compromise and option 4 offers alternatives. After a deep enough change, "The old map of possibilities ceases to be a useful map" (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5).
- **History offers examples.** The essay names industrialisation, the digital revolution and major wars as shifts that reordered earlier disputes (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5).
- **Earlier concerns remain.** Seefeld says they do not disappear: "they become subordinate to a larger reality" (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 4).
- **Finance has its own polarities.** He lists philanthropy versus investment, commercial returns versus impact, and shareholder versus stakeholder value (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5). Under a resilience frame these start to look odd as sides to pick (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5).
- **Why foundational economies matter.** The essay gives one reason: they sustain the conditions on which all other value depends (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 5).
- **Two caveats.** The essay calls itself a simplification of diverse political identities. It also says the exercise asks for cognitive flexibility (raw/The 5 Options of our Time.pdf, p. 1).
